The step from senior manager to director is where many strong performers stall. Nothing is wrong with their work. The job simply changes shape, and the habits that earned the last promotion stop being the ones that earn the next.

## What changes

As a senior manager you are judged mostly on delivery: your team ships, your projects land, your people are happy. At director level you are judged on whether the whole area is pointed at the right things, and whether it will still work when you are not in the room.

## Five shifts that matter

1. **From delivering to deciding what is worth delivering.** Say no to good work so the best work gets room.
2. **From your team to the organisation.** Directors are expected to see across teams, resolve conflicts between them, and think about the whole system.
3. **From being the answer to building leaders.** If everything still routes through you, you are the ceiling. Develop managers who can make the calls.
4. **From activity to a narrative.** Leaders above you need a short, clear story of what your area is achieving and why it matters, told in terms of outcomes.
5. **From waiting to be noticed to being visible.** Sponsors can only back what they can see. Make your impact easy to describe by others.

## Questions worth asking yourself

- What am I still doing that a manager of mine should be doing?
- Who senior to me could describe my impact in two sentences, and would they say the same thing I would?
- What would I stop doing if I trusted my team more?

## Where coaching helps

These shifts are rarely about knowledge. They are about letting go of what has worked, getting clear on where you want to go, and acting on it consistently. A coach gives you a place to think it through and someone to keep you moving.
