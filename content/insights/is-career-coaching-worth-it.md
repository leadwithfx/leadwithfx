Coaching costs real money and real time, so it is fair to ask whether it is worth it. The honest answer is that it depends on you and on the coach.

## What coaching can do

- Help you get clear on what you actually want, instead of what you think you should want.
- Turn a vague sense of being stuck into specific next steps.
- Build the habits behind a move, such as visibility, difficult conversations or delegation.
- Give you a confidential space to think with someone who is on your side but not invested in your decision.

## What it cannot do

- It cannot supply motivation you do not have. You do the work between sessions.
- It will not hand you answers. A good coach asks, they do not prescribe.
- It will not guarantee a promotion, a new job or any other result. Be wary of anyone who promises one.

## Who gets the most from it

People who are capable and doing well on paper but unsure of the next move, who are stepping into a bigger role, or who are changing direction. It tends to work best when you come with a real decision or goal in mind and are willing to be challenged.

## How to choose a coach

Whoever you pick, ask:

1. What training do you have, and what is still in progress?
2. What code of conduct do you work to?
3. How do you keep what I share confidential?
4. What does a session look like?
5. Is there a free first call with no hard sell?

## How long does it take?

One conversation can bring clarity, but lasting change usually needs a few months. Four sessions spaced over four months is a common pattern, because it leaves time to try things between sessions.
