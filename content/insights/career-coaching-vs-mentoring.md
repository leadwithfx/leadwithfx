People use "coach" and "mentor" as if they mean the same thing. They do not, and picking the wrong one is the most common reason help does not help.

## What a mentor does

A mentor has walked a path you want to walk. They share what worked for them, open doors, and warn you about the potholes. The value is their experience.

The limit is that their experience is theirs. What worked in their company, in their decade, with their manager may not fit your situation.

## What a coach does

A coach does not need to have done your job. Their skill is asking the questions that help you see your own situation clearly, then helping you commit to a next step and follow through. The value is the process, and the answers stay yours.

A coach will not tell you what to do. If you want a recommendation, a mentor or consultant is the better fit.

## How to choose

- **You want to copy a proven path.** Find a mentor.
- **You are unsure which path is yours.** Work with a coach.
- **You know what to do and keep not doing it.** Work with a coach.
- **You need a specific skill fast.** Hire a trainer or consultant.

## Using both

They work well together. A mentor tells you how the terrain looks. A coach helps you decide where you actually want to go and holds you to it.

## For tech leaders and career changers

Technical people often default to finding a mentor, because a mentor feels like information. But the hard parts of a move into leadership, such as letting go of doing the work yourself, handling influence without authority, and deciding what you want next, are rarely information problems. They are clarity and habit problems, which is where coaching helps most.
